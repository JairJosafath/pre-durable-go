Why durable

A Lambda function that outlives its invocation

The workflow stays in one handler, in ordinary Go. Completed work is checkpointed, so a restart resumes instead of starting over.

0:30 to 1:30. No clicks: the panel plays once, about 12 seconds, and holds on its last frame. Start from the pain while the bar grows: a multi-step process (charge, wait for approval, ship) does not fit in one 15-minute invocation, so we normally split it across queues, state tables and a workflow service, and write the retry and resume logic ourselves. When the bar hits the 15 minute limit, say that is the wall. Then the axis zooms out from minutes to a year: a durable function is a Lambda function created with a DurableConfig; Lambda checkpoints each completed operation and, when the function is interrupted or suspends, invokes it again and resumes. The blue ticks are short invocations; the orange stretches are waits that use no compute. Four benefits to name: reliable progress, long-running workflows, no compute while suspended, and no duplicate side effects on replay. Cost note if asked: you pay for compute, requests, durable operations (each is a checkpoint), and stored state.
