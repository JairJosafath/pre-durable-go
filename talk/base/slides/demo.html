14:00 to 19:00, five minutes. The stages and operation names are the ones in internal/pipeline. Script: 1) Infra, 45 seconds. Open infra/lib/constructs/upload-pipeline.ts. durableConfig is the only infrastructure difference between a durable function and a regular one: execution timeout is the 24 hour review timeout plus one hour, retention is 14 days, while the function timeout for a single invocation is one minute. A durable function is always invoked through a version or alias, here "live", so an execution that started on one version keeps replaying on that version even if you deploy while it waits. S3 invokes the alias directly for keys under users/. 2) Upload a photo in the frontend (presigned URL). Use one that needs review: a moderation flag, or no label at 80 percent confidence. A clean photo is approved without waiting and goes straight to indexing. 3) Open the execution: register-upload, detect-labels, detect-moderation and generate-thumbnail are checkpointed, and it is waiting at human-review. The analysis ran on its own goroutine with durable.Go while durable.InvokeAsync called the thumbnailer; durable.Join awaited both. Point out that no invocation is running. 4) Approve with POST /reviews. The reviewer Lambda reads the callback ID from the file record and calls SendDurableExecutionCallbackSuccess. The same execution resumes: replay skips everything already checkpointed, then durable.Map indexes each label in its own step and mark-ready sets the status to READY. 5) Search with GET /query?index=<label> and show the photo. 6) If time allows, say what happens on reject or after 24 hours without a decision: durable.Parallel deletes the original and the thumbnail and the record is marked REJECTED. If a step fails for good, the record is marked FAILED. Mention that catalog items are keyed by what they describe, so the index step is safe to retry and replay. Keep a finished execution open in another tab in case the live one misbehaves.
